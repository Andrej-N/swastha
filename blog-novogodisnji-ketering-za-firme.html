New Year Catering for Companies in Belgrade: How to Plan the Office Party

Somewhere around the middle of November, someone in the office realises that nobody has organised the New Year party. By then the good dates are going, the venue is asking for a deposit and the caterer is already juggling a full December. The party itself is not hard to plan. It only becomes hard when the first decision gets made in the last week. A New Year event for a company comes down to a date, a place, a realistic headcount and a menu built for how people will actually eat that evening.

Book the Date Before You Plan the Menu

The middle two weeks of December are when most companies want the same Thursday and Friday evenings. Treat the date as the first decision, and if you can, keep a second date in mind. An early week evening or the first week of December is often easier to get and just as festive. A team party does not have to happen in the last days before the holidays, when half the office is already gone. The menu can wait a little. The date cannot.

Office or Venue: What Changes for the Food

A party in your own office is cheaper on rent and harder on logistics. Ask the practical questions early: is there a lift, where can a caterer unload, is there a table long enough, who clears up, and can anything be kept warm? A rented venue solves some of that, but check whether it allows outside catering at all, and whether it charges for it. Many restaurants and event spaces work only with their own kitchen, and it is much better to find that out before you pay a deposit.

How Much Food a New Year Party Actually Needs

The hour on the invitation decides the quantity more than the guest list does. A party that starts at six or seven in the evening falls straight across dinner, and people arrive hungry from work. As a starting point, count on 6 to 8 pieces per person for a short gathering of about two hours with other food to follow, and on 12 to 15 pieces per person, or a warm dish on top, when the party is the only dinner they will get. For thirty people, that is the difference between about 240 and about 450 pieces. Running out at nine o'clock, with drinks still being poured, is the one thing guests will remember.

Food That Works With a Glass in One Hand

Most New Year parties are eaten standing up. That makes finger food the natural format, and it also sets a simple rule: everything should be one or two bites, need no knife, and not fall apart on the way to someone's mouth. Add something warm, because it is December and because a table of cold canapés starts to feel thin after an hour. Small cups of soup work surprisingly well in winter. So does a warm dish served in the second half of the evening, when the room has settled and people are ready for something more substantial.

The Guests Most December Menus Forget

In Serbia the Nativity Fast runs from November 28 to January 6, which means some of your colleagues will be fasting at the party, and they usually will not say so in advance. If the only thing they can eat is a bowl of crisps, they notice, and so does everyone else. Plan a real share of the table without meat, dairy or eggs and label it clearly. Those dishes will also cover your vegan guests and many people with dairy allergies. Ask about other dietary needs on the invitation, not at the door. We wrote more about this in our guides to fasting menus and allergies and special diets.

"Nobody remembers the fourth canapé. Everybody remembers the moment the food ran out."

A Simple Timeline From November to the Party

You can run the whole thing on four dates. By mid November, settle the date, the place and a rough headcount, and ask for a proposal. By the end of November, confirm the menu and sort out who brings drinks, glasses and tableware. One week before, send a final reminder and collect dietary needs. Two days before, give the caterer the confirmed number and the exact delivery time, along with the name and phone number of the person who will be there to receive everything. That last detail sounds minor until a van is waiting outside a locked building at five in the afternoon.

Questions to Ask Before You Confirm

Before you say yes, ask: what exactly is included, and what is charged separately? Does the price cover delivery, setup and collection of equipment, or only the food? Until when can the headcount change? Is anyone staying during the event, or is everything delivered and left ready? How much of the menu is suitable for fasting guests? How is food kept warm in a room without a kitchen? Two offers can look very different until you compare them on these points. The Bottom Line

A good New Year party needs an early date, an honest quantity and a menu that includes everyone at the table.
