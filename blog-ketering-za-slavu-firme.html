Catering for a Company Slava: How to Plan the Table in the Office

A slava is one of the few occasions when a company is a host in the old sense of the word. Partners, clients, neighbours and former colleagues drop by to wish you well, and the table is what they see. It is also unlike any other company event: there is no start time, no seating plan and no way to know exactly who will come. When a business searches for slava catering, it is usually not asking for a recipe. It needs someone to make sure the table still looks generous at four in the afternoon, in an office with no kitchen.

A Slava Is an Open House, Not a Party

A party has a peak. A slava has waves. Guests arrive over many hours, usually stay for a short while, and often come in small groups between meetings. That changes almost everything about the food. It should not all go out at once. It needs to hold up well over several hours, or be replenished in batches, and a guest who arrives at five should get the same welcome as one who arrived at noon. Ask your caterer to plan in rounds, with fresh trays held back for later, rather than one big table that slowly falls apart.

Posna or Mrsna: Check the Calendar First

The first question about any slava menu is whether the table will be posna or mrsna, and the date decides it. Slavas that fall during a fast, or on a Wednesday or Friday, are traditionally posne. Nikoljdan on December 19 falls in the Nativity Fast and is served with fish, while Aranđelovdan on November 21 comes just before the fast begins. In 2026 both of those fall on a Saturday, so decide early whether the office will mark the day itself or host guests on a working day. If you are not sure what applies to your slava, ask your priest, then give the caterer a clear answer. Our guide to posni catering explains what the terms mean for a kitchen.

What the Host Keeps and What the Caterer Takes

The heart of a slava is not a catering item. The slavski kolač, the žito, the candle and the wine, and the moment with the priest, belong to the host and the tradition, and many families and companies prefer to prepare or order these themselves. Agree clearly if you want any of that handled for you, and do not assume it is included. Everything around it can be handed over: the dishes, the delivery, the platters, the replenishing during the day. That leaves the people hosting free to do what a slava actually asks of them, which is to stand at the door and greet their guests.

What Belongs on a Slava Table in an Office

Most guests at a company slava stand, greet the host, eat something and leave for their next meeting. They need food that can be eaten neatly on a small plate. A mrsna table usually means cold starters, cheese and proja, a warm dish such as sarma or roast meat, and cakes. A posna table can be just as full: smoked or baked fish, posna sarma, prebranac, baked peppers, ajvar, pickles, walnut and fruit based sweets. Keep tradition where guests expect it, and let the rest be lighter. Plenty of people visit several slavas in one week, and they are often glad to see a fresh salad.

"A slava table is judged at four in the afternoon, not at eleven in the morning."

How Much Food for Guests You Cannot Count

No one sends an RSVP for a slava, but you can still estimate. Look at who came last year, or make a list of people you expect and how many of them normally turn up. Add the team, who will eat throughout the day. Because each guest stays a short while, most of them eat less than at a sit down lunch, and a fair starting point is about half to two thirds of a full meal per expected guest. Order in rounds so the last one can be adjusted, and agree in advance what happens to leftovers. Sending them home with colleagues is a perfectly good end to the day.

Getting the Office Ready

A few small things make the day run properly. Clear one long table away from the main walkway, because guests will gather there. Keep a separate spot for the host's table with the kolač and the candle, and leave space around it. Find a cool room or a working fridge for the next round of trays. Tell reception and building security that guests will be coming all day. Have plates, glasses and bins ready before the first guest arrives, and agree who is collecting used plates as the day goes on. If your slava falls during working hours, decide in advance who covers the phones.

The Bottom Line

A company slava works when the food follows the rhythm of the day: rounds instead of one big table, a clear decision on posna or mrsna, and a host who is free to greet guests.
