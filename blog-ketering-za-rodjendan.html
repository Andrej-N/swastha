Birthday Catering in Belgrade: Menu Ideas, Quantities and Planning for the Office and Home

A birthday is the one celebration everybody understands, and the one where the host most wants to be at the table instead of in the kitchen. Whether it is a colleague's birthday in the office, a round fiftieth at home or an eighteenth with forty friends, catering takes the cooking off your hands, but only if you order the right format and the right amount. This guide covers menu ideas, quantities and the small decisions that make a birthday table work from the first guest to the last.

Three Kinds of Birthday, Three Different Orders

An office birthday is short and happens during working hours: 15 to 40 people, often a break of 30 to 60 minutes. Finger food and a cake work best, and nothing should need cutlery. A birthday at home is longer, with family and friends who sit down, so the food has to last through the evening; cold platters with one or two warm dishes, or a small buffet, are the usual choice. A big birthday, such as an eighteenth, thirtieth or fiftieth in a rented space, should be planned like an event, with a cocktail or buffet, staff and equipment.

Menu Ideas That Work

  • Cold platters: cheese and cured meats, vegetables with dips, mini sandwiches and tortilla rolls. They are the base of almost every birthday table.
  • One warm dish: chicken skewers, baked pasta, stuffed peppers or a vegetable lasagne. One warm dish done well beats four lukewarm ones.
  • Salads that survive a few hours: grain salads, roasted vegetables or chickpea salad, rather than leafy greens that wilt by the second hour.
  • Something light: fresh fruit, a plant based option and at least one dish without gluten, so that no guest goes home hungry.

For a children's birthday, keep it simple and familiar: small sandwiches, mini pizzas, chicken pieces, vegetable sticks and fruit, and leave out whole nuts and hard round pieces for the youngest guests. Ask parents about allergies when you send the invitation, not on the day. Our article on allergies and special diets in catering explains how a professional kitchen handles this.

How Much Food for a Birthday

Count by the length of the celebration and the time of day. For a two hour office break, plan 6 to 8 finger food pieces per person plus the cake. For an evening at home that replaces dinner, plan 12 to 15 pieces per adult or, for a buffet, about 350 to 450 g of food per adult in total across platters, the warm dish and salads. Children eat roughly half an adult portion. Add a margin of 5 to 10 percent for a buffet, but not for individually packed portions. For worked totals by group size, see how much food to order for 10, 20, 30 or 50 people.

The Cake Is a Separate Decision

Most caterers, including us, focus on savoury food, and the birthday cake is usually ordered from a confectioner. Plan the two together. Agree the time the cake will be cut, so that the savoury table is not cleared too early, and keep a cool place and some free space ready for it. A small selection of sweet bites next to the cake is a good idea for guests who do not eat it.

Office Birthdays: The Small Details

In the office, one person usually organises and everybody else is surprised. Order for the number of people who will actually be in that day, choose formats that need no plates, and agree a delivery time about 15 minutes before the break. Ask for compostable plates and napkins if your office has none. If birthdays are frequent, some companies hold one monthly birthday lunch for everyone born that month, which is simpler, fairer and cheaper than separate orders.

Birthdays at Home: Space and Timing

At home, the limit is rarely the food and almost always the space. Before you order, look at where the table will stand, how much room the fridge has for cold platters and whether the oven is free to keep a warm dish hot. Ask the caterer to deliver cold food about an hour before the guests arrive and the warm dish as late as possible, ideally in a container that keeps heat. Platters that can be served straight from the packaging save you an hour of washing up after everyone has gone home.

What Affects the Price

The price of birthday catering depends on the number of guests, the format, the number of warm dishes, the ingredients, whether staff and equipment are needed and the delivery time. Weekend evenings and the holiday season can cost more and fill up earlier. Ask for an offer that lists pieces or grams per person, so you know exactly what you are paying for. Our article on catering prices per person explains the rest.

The Bottom Line

Choose the format by the kind of birthday, count your food from the clock, keep one good warm dish and plan the cake separately.
